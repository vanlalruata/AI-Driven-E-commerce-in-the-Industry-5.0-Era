\section*{Response to the Second Round of Editor's / Reviewers' Comments}
We are deeply grateful to the editor and reviewers for their follow-up feedback on our revised manuscript. Below, we address each comment point-by-point and describe the corresponding updates we have made in this second revision. All new changes are highlighted in \update{blue} in the revised manuscript.

\subsection*{Point-by-Point Responses to Reviewer \#1}

\comment{\textbf{Comment 1: Reframing to Parallel-Empirical Framework}} \\
The datasets are disjoint, with 0.00\% ASIN overlap and a 10-year temporal mismatch. The manuscript should be reframed to acknowledge this parallel structure and methodological caution, rather than claiming an integrated empirical framework.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for this excellent recommendation to reframe the study. We have fully accepted this recommendation. The manuscript, including the title, abstract, and introduction, has been reframed to emphasise a \update{Parallel-Empirical Framework and Methodological Caution}. We explicitly caution researchers and practitioners against assuming seamless integration of secondary digital retail streams in emerging markets. The two analytics modules—sentiment classification benchmarking on the review corpus and operational/concentration analytics on the sales dataset—are now presented as cooperative parallel components connected through a decision support matrix, preserving empirical validity without implying unsupported causal linkages.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 2: Refinement of Hypotheses}} \\
The hypotheses should be limited strictly to tested mathematical/statistical relationships, removing conceptual hypotheses related to Industry 5.0 or DPDP compliance that are not directly tested.

\response
We are grateful to the reviewer for highlighting the need to refine our hypotheses. We agree. We have removed the conceptual hypotheses from Section 1. The formal hypotheses are now limited strictly to evaluated mathematical and statistical relationships:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \update{\textit{Hypothesis H1a/H1b}: Model classification performance, stability, and calibration across architectures under class imbalance.}
    \item \update{\textit{Hypothesis H2a/H2b}: Geographic/SKU sales concentration (Gini/HHI) and cross-marketplace pricing consistency (Kruskal-Wallis/Dunn-Bonferroni).}
    \item \update{\textit{Hypothesis H3}: Data linkage audit disjointness (ASIN catalog overlap).}
\end{itemize}
Industry 5.0 and DPDP compliance implications are now framed strictly as interpretive translations and managerial guidelines rather than direct empirical findings.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 3: Fulfilment Profitability and Figure 13}} \\
Shorten the fulfilment profitability section to highlight database API limitations rather than a comparative empirical analysis, given that Shiprocket has zero valid rows ($N=0$), and update Figure 13.

\response
We thank the reviewer for this constructive suggestion regarding the fulfilment analysis. We have shortened Section 4.5 (renamed to \update{Data-Quality Audit of Multi-Channel Fulfilment Integration}) and reframed it as an empirical audit finding. We discuss how database constraints and API integration failures lead to $N=0$ for Shiprocket. Figure 13 has been updated and copied to the figures folder; it now shows only the INCREFF profit distribution, while the text and caption explain the blank comparator for Shiprocket as a data-quality warning.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 4: DPDP and Governance Separation}} \\
Distinguish clearly between direct empirical results (disjoint datasets, pricing consistency) and recommended governance guidelines in the DPDP section.

\response
We appreciate the reviewer's valuable suggestion to distinguish empirical results from governance guidelines. In Section 5, we have separated our discussion into distinct subsections:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \update{\textbf{Subsection 5.1 (Empirical Constraints and Regulatory Risks)}: Details the compliance risks under the DPDP Act of 2023 that arise from disjoint catalogs (ASIN mismatches) and centralized marketplace price synchronization.}
    \item \update{\textbf{Subsection 5.2 (Managerial Guidelines for DPDP Compliance)}: Lays out specific, normative engineering and governance controls (consent capture, PII anonymization, cryptographic logs) to address these risks.}
\end{itemize}

\comment{\textbf{Comment 5: Formatting Guidelines}} \\
I strongly recommend using APA 7th referencing style consistently and formatting the manuscript in Times New Roman, 12-point font, with consistent margins, headings, table formatting, figure captions, and reference style.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for this guidance on manuscript formatting. The manuscript has been formatted using a 12-point Times font (equivalent to Times New Roman) by specifying the \update{12pt} and \update{times} options in the document class, ensuring consistent margins, headings, table structures, and reference style. \update{Additionally, we have significantly shortened all figure captions throughout the manuscript to make them concise and descriptive, while moving the detailed empirical interpretations and diagnostic discussions directly into the main body paragraphs (such as in Section 4.1, 4.3, 4.4, and 4.7) to improve readability and parity.} Furthermore, the bibliography and in-text citations have been updated to strictly and consistently follow the APA 7th referencing style, as recommended.

\subsection*{Point-by-Point Responses to Reviewer \#2}

\comment{\textbf{Comment 1: Empirical Integration vs. Parallel Analyses}} \\
The study lacks genuine empirical integration between sentiment analytics and sales analytics, resulting in two parallel analyses rather than a unified framework.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for highlighting the need to clarify the empirical relationship between sentiment and sales analytics. In alignment with this concern and similar feedback from Reviewer \#1, we have fully reframed the study as a \update{Parallel-Empirical Framework and Methodological Caution}. We explicitly acknowledge that the sentiment analysis on reviews and operational/concentration analytics on transactional sales are distinct, parallel streams of analysis. Rather than claiming an integrated empirical link, we demonstrate how these parallel insights cooperatively support decision-making when mapped together in an operational decision support matrix, preserving the statistical integrity of the disjoint datasets.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 2: Industry 5.0 Operationalization}} \\
Provide a concrete operationalization table mapping Industry 5.0 pillars (human-centricity, sustainability, resilience) to empirical metrics or thresholds.

\response
We thank the reviewer for this constructive recommendation to operationalise these Industry 5.0 pillars. We have added a new subsection and table in Section 2.1: \update{Table 1 (Industry 5.0 Empirical Translation and Operationalisation Matrix)}. This table maps:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \update{\textit{Human-Centricity} to the human-in-the-loop decision-support buffer ($0.4 < P(pos) < 0.6$).}
    \item \update{\textit{Sustainability} to algorithmic complexity (linear $O(N \cdot L)$ vs. quadratic $O(N \cdot L^2 \cdot D \cdot H)$) and CPU vs. GPU resource demands.}
    \item \update{\textit{Resilience} to SKU and geographical concentration indices (Gini thresholds of 0.6843 and 0.8169).}
\end{itemize}

\comment{\textbf{Comment 3: AI Methodological Contribution and Algorithmic Novelty}} \\
The methodological contribution to artificial intelligence is limited because the study primarily benchmarks existing machine learning and deep learning models such as Naïve Bayes, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, SVM, and DistilBERT without proposing a novel algorithm, hybrid architecture, optimisation strategy, or innovative feature engineering approach.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for this insightful comment regarding the algorithmic novelty of our sentiment classification models. We agree that our study does not introduce a completely new deep learning architecture or algorithm from scratch. However, in the context of Industry 5.0, which prioritises environmental sustainability (Green AI) and accessibility for resource-constrained small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in emerging markets, developing highly complex, energy-intensive models is often counterproductive. Instead, the methodological contribution of our work lies in the \update{applied engineering pipeline} designed to make computationally lightweight models (like SVM and TF-IDF+LR) operationally reliable and robust under a severe class imbalance (5.41:1). Specifically, we introduce:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \update{\textbf{Platt-Scaling Probability Calibration}}: Utilizing a 3-fold cross-validation calibration (\texttt{CalibratedClassifierCV}) for SVM and logistic sigmoid fitting for Logistic Regression to map raw decision boundaries to reliable posterior probabilities $P(pos)$ (validated via Brier scores).
    \item \update{\textbf{Human-in-the-Loop Uncertainty Routing}}: Defining a formal decision-support boundary ($0.4 < P(pos) < 0.6$) where predictively ambiguous reviews are routed to human moderators, preserving human agency as mandated by Industry 5.0.
    \item \update{\textbf{Sublinear Term Frequency Scaling}}: Mitigating document length bias in TF-IDF by replacing raw frequencies with $1 + \log(tf)$ to stabilize decision boundaries.
\end{itemize}
We have expanded Section 3.3 to include a detailed pseudocode of the Sentiment NLP Pipeline (Algorithm 1) and added the proposed architecture diagram (Figure 1) to clarify these details, demonstrating how a calibrated and sustainable pipeline provides a robust engineering contribution without the computational/carbon footprint of massive transformers.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 4: Concrete Decision Support System}} \\
The manuscript lacks a concrete decision support system showing routing rules or escalation logic.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for highlighting this and recommending a concrete decision support matrix. We have added a new subsection, \update{Section 4.6 (Operational Decision Support Matrix and Escalation Logic)}, and formalised its routing procedures in the Operational Decision-Support Routing Framework (Algorithm 2). This section presents a concrete routing matrix (\update{Table 8}) that maps calibrated sentiment probabilities against Gini concentration thresholds to route customer reviews to automated CRM ticketing, a human-in-the-loop buffer, or immediate supply-chain manager escalation.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 5: Managerial Relevance and Real-world Validation}} \\
The managerial relevance remains largely speculative because the proposed framework is not validated through real-world business applications such as retailer case studies, manager interviews, user experiments, or organisational implementation.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for pointing out the importance of validating our findings in real-world environments. We have added a new paragraph in our conclusion section (Section 7), discussing explicitly acknowledging this limitation. We discuss that while our parallel-empirical framework provides a statistical blueprint, validating its managerial relevance through retailer case studies, qualitative manager interviews, user experiments, or live organisational implementation represents a critical next step for future work.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 6: Generalisability and Multi-Platform Replication}} \\
The external validity and generalisability of the findings are limited, as the empirical analysis relies on a single review dataset and a single transactional dataset without replication across other major Indian e-commerce platforms such as Flipkart, Myntra, Meesho, or ONDC.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for raising the critical issue of external validity and generalisability across Indian marketplaces. We have revised the Conclusion and future directions (Section 7) to explicitly note the limitation of relying on a single review dataset and transactional sales dataset. We detail that replicating this parallel analysis across other major Indian e-commerce networks—such as Flipkart, Myntra, Meesho, or live ONDC transaction logs—is essential to confirm the external validity and generalisability of our sales concentration and pricing findings.

\comment{\textbf{Comment 7: Deployment Barriers and ONDC}} \\
Discuss the barriers to SME implementation (API overhead, human costs) and ONDC's role in standardization and replication.

\response
We sincerely thank the reviewer for this valuable guidance regarding deployment barriers and ONDC's standardising role. We have added a new subsection, \update{Subsection 5.3 (Barriers to Deployment and ONDC's Standardising Role)}, which details the software engineering and maintenance overhead of third-party API integration, the labor cost of maintaining a human-in-the-loop buffer, and explains how ONDC's open network protocols serve as a democratizing force to standardize schemas and reduce integration barriers for SMEs.

\vspace{18pt}
\noindent We believe that this thorough revision comprehensively addresses all the concerns, doubts, and questions raised by the reviewers. We are confident that these improvements have significantly strengthened the manuscript, and we hope it is now suitable for publication.
